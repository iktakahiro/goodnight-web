## What it is

goodnight-web gives a small company the three things most company sites need:

- **Pages and a blog.** An Astro site with a home page, company profile, services, news, blog, contact page, and privacy policy, in Japanese and English. Blog posts are Markdown files in the repository.
- **A contact form.** Submissions are checked with Turnstile, rate limited, and saved to D1 before the visitor sees a reply.
- **An admin screen for inquiries.** Admins sign in with a passkey, read inquiries, mark them unread or read, search them by email address, check suspected spam apart from the rest, delete them, and export them as CSV.

Everything runs on Cloudflare: one Worker with Static Assets and one D1 database. The only other service is GitHub, where your copy of the site lives. A Slack notification is optional.

## Who it is for

- A developer or a small web agency that sets up and hands over sites for clients.
- A person at a small company or startup who looks after the company site, often without an engineering background, and edits it with AI tools.

You do not need to write server code. The contact API and the admin screen come from an npm package that you do not edit.

## What you still maintain

goodnight-web removes server and runtime maintenance. Some maintenance remains, and you should plan for it:

- **Review and merge dependency update pull requests.** Fixes to goodnight-web arrive as Dependabot pull requests in your repository. Nobody merges them for you.
- **Update the site's own dependencies**, such as Astro and Tailwind CSS. These also arrive as Dependabot pull requests.
- **Follow Cloudflare changes**, such as a new `compatibility_date` or a change to a free plan limit.
- **Keep your repository private** and your Worker secrets safe.
- **Check your privacy policy** with a professional before you publish it.
